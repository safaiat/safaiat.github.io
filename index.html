Making the unseen measurable.

Tomography reveals internal structure without cutting into an object. Turning measurements into a detailed three-dimensional image, however, requires solving a demanding inverse problem—one that becomes increasingly expensive as resolution, data size, and model complexity grow.

My research brings imaging physics, AI, and high-performance computing together to tackle that computational cost. Using the Frontier supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, I investigate scalable reconstruction methods aimed at recovering reliable images while making advanced imaging computationally practical.

Why the image matters

Inspect without destruction.

Three-dimensional imaging supports the study of materials, engineered components, and biological specimens while preserving what is being examined.

Beyond physical structure.

Emission imaging adds information about radiotracer distributions, extending reconstruction questions from physical structure to biological function.

Make complexity tractable.

High-resolution volumes and physically detailed models demand substantial memory and computation. Scalable methods help address this growing computational burden.
