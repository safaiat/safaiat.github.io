About me

My research lies at the intersection of computational imaging, statistical inverse problems, machine learning, and high-performance computing.

I am pursuing a Ph.D. in Data Science and Engineering at the Bredesen Center, University of Tennessee, Knoxville, beginning in August 2024. My doctoral research is supervised by Dr. Xiao Wang.

At the University of Tennessee and in collaboration with researchers at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, I work on scalable reconstruction algorithms for CT and SPECT imaging. I am particularly interested in methods that combine accurate imaging physics, advanced optimization, and learned priors.

My work brings together imaging physics, statistical reasoning, and scientific computing to address challenging reconstruction problems.

Before specializing in computational imaging, I worked on computer vision for infrastructure assessment, EEG-based character recognition, generative texture synthesis, Bengali hand-sign recognition, and efficient neural network architectures.

My broader interests include image priors, computational inverse problems, and large-scale scientific computing.
