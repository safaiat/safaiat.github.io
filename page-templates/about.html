About me

I work at the intersection of computational imaging, statistical inverse problems, machine learning, and high-performance computing.

I am a Ph.D. researcher in Data Science and Engineering at the Bredesen Center, University of Tennessee, Knoxville, where I began my doctoral studies in August 2024. My research is supervised by Dr. Xiao Wang and involves collaboration with researchers at Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

My doctoral work focuses on recovering internal structure and radiotracer distributions from physical measurements. These images offer complementary views of anatomy and biological activity. The challenge is to recover useful information despite noise, incomplete observations, and the computational demands of three-dimensional reconstruction.

I use the Frontier supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory to investigate GPU-based reconstruction at a scale that requires substantial computing resources. This connects my interests in imaging physics, mathematical methods, and AI with the practical challenge of making advanced reconstruction computationally feasible.

Academic foundation

I earned my B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from Bangladesh Army University of Engineering and Technology (BAUET), Bangladesh. My academic background connects electrical engineering with signal analysis, mathematical modeling, and computational methods.

This foundation in electrical engineering supports my work with measurement systems and computational analysis. It also connects my earlier signal-processing and computer-vision projects with the imaging questions I study today.

Earlier research

My earlier published work spans several applications of machine learning. In infrastructure assessment, I worked on pothole detection and repair-cost estimation and crack and dampness detection in buildings, using real-world imagery from Bangladesh.

Other projects include English-character recognition, StyleGAN2 texture generation, lightweight Bengali hand-sign recognition, and biologically inspired neural-network training. This work provides a broader foundation in computer vision, signal analysis, and generative modeling alongside my current imaging research.
